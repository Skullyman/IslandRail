The Practicality, Affordability and Long-Term Value of Restoring Passenger Rail

Our "behaviour-first" study looks at how Islanders actually travel — weighing time, cost and frequency — and projects a stabilized demand of about 1.55 million passenger trips a year by 2040, driven mainly by commuting in the Victoria–Langford–Nanaimo corridor. It outlines how Land Value Capture districts and Indigenous Equity Trusts could let government "dial" its support, and frames the corridor as generational community infrastructure, like highways, hospitals and water systems.

One Island + One Corridor = Two Futures

Bill MacGougan · Substack · July 2026

An essay on the decision Vancouver Island is making about its rail corridor, often without realizing it. MacGougan sets the Island's future with restored rail beside its future without it, reading each at ten, twenty and fifty years out, and argues for returning rail to the full corridor. Especially worthwhile for decision makers.

Enhancing Rail Transit in Canada — railtransit.ca

Gwyer Webber

Gwyer Webber's site of ideas for rail transit across Canada, with detailed proposals for Vancouver Island. His Island plan preserves the corridor for intercity rail, regional rail, freight and a multi-use trail, and compares the per-kilometre cost of upgrading the rail line against recent highway bus-lane projects. For the Capital Region he sets out two tunnel-based options to bring trains into Victoria.
